About the workshop

Social intelligence is the next frontier for autonomous driving.

Safe navigation alone is not enough for autonomous vehicles to integrate seamlessly into mixed-traffic environments.

Autonomous vehicles must interpret implicit traffic norms, infer human intentions, and take part in cooperative or competitive interactions. This workshop examines how strategic modeling, adaptive learning, and rigorous control can work together to meet that challenge.

Through keynote presentations, invited talks, and a panel discussion, experts in multi-agent systems, reinforcement learning, human factors, and formal methods will connect high-level reasoning with robust, deployable vehicle architectures. The aim is to identify a practical research agenda for autonomous vehicles that are not only intelligent, but also socially adept and trustworthy.

Topics of interest

Core questions for socially aware autonomy

The workshop connects complementary perspectives across interaction, intelligence, and assurance.

Human-vehicle interaction modeling

Understanding social cues, driving conventions, and behavioral adaptation in mixed traffic.

Game-theoretic planning

Reasoning about cooperation, competition, and interdependent road-user decisions.

Safe reinforcement learning

Combining learning and adaptation with constraints for dependable vehicle behavior.

Uncertainty-aware decision-making

Planning robustly when perception, prediction, and human intent remain uncertain.

Real-world validation

Evaluating integrated approaches under realistic conditions and deployment constraints.
